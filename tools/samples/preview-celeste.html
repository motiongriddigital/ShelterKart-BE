Three questions to start with

Put these to the developer in writing. Each says who should answer and what a good answer looks like.

  1. Q1 Ask the legal team, before booking

    The registration names Nayan Real Estate Private Limited as promoter, while the project is sold as Hubtown. Which company signs my agreement for sale, issues my receipts and holds the title? Please give its CIN and registered office in writing.

    Why it matters. You can only enforce your agreement against the company that signs it. A brand creates no obligation.

    A good answer
    A signed letter naming one company with its CIN, matching the RERA certificate and the draft agreement.
    Does not close it
    "It is all Hubtown" or a group chart in a brochure.

  2. Q2 Ask the promoters, before booking

    Hubtown's Managing Director and a Hubtown director appear on Nayan Real Estate's filing. Will Hubtown Limited sign an enforceable guarantee of completion and of Nayan's obligations under my agreement, including refunds and delay interest?

    Why it matters. Common control is not legal liability. Without a signed guarantee the listed parent owes you nothing if the project company fails.

    A good answer
    A guarantee or undertaking executed by Hubtown Limited and annexed to the agreement, reviewed by your lawyer.
    Does not close it
    A verbal assurance or a press release.

  3. Q3 Ask the legal team, before booking

    The project was registered in August 2017 and its filed completion date is 31 July 2029. What was the original completion date, how many times has it been extended, and did the existing allottees consent to each extension as MahaRERA requires?

    Why it matters. Twelve years from registration to completion is long. Each extension, and whether buyers consented, shows how the project has been run and what your date is worth.

    A good answer
    The original certificate, every extension order and the allottee-consent records.
    Does not close it
    "Covid delays" without the orders.
