How we grade evidence

Every claim in a report is graded by how well it is supported, and shown with the pages it came from. The grade is decided by code, from the sources themselves, never by the AI that read them.

The grades

Confirmed at source. Found on an official source: a regulator, a company or insolvency register, a court, or a stock-exchange filing.

Reported by several sites. Said by two or more independent websites. Several pages on the same website count once.

Single source. Found on one website only. Shown to you, and marked as single-sourced until a second independent source agrees.

Developer’s own claim. From the developer’s own website, brochure or social accounts, or marketing repeated by agents. Shown to you as the developer’s claim; it never raises a finding or a question.

Sources disagree. Where sources give different answers — two possession dates, three prices — every version is shown side by side, with where each came from, instead of choosing one.

What is checked, and what is removed

A claim is kept only if a page the research actually retrieved supports it; anything else is removed before you see the report, and the report says how many were removed, if any. Websites named after a project are often run by estate agents rather than the developer, and are treated that way. Posts on social media and forums are individual views, and are labelled as such.

When something could not be found, the report says so in plain words and tells you who to ask, rather than filling the gap with a guess. See also how a report is built.

Official registers and their licences

Where a register confirms a developer company at source, the report names it, and the foot of every report credits the registers whose data it contains. Some of these licences make that credit a condition of reuse.
