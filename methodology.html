How a report is built

This text is available in English only. The English version governs.

A report is built from public sources. Every finding links to the page it came from and shows the date that page was read.

What every report answers

The developer. Its other projects, and what the public record says about them.

The project. Registration, approvals, dates and progress, as the official sources state them.

Legal standing. Any official register entry, court record or regulator action we can find.

What buyers say. Themes that recur across independent reviews, forums and social media, shown as what people say, not as fact.

What the developer discloses. The facts the developer publishes itself, marked as the developer’s own claim.

How each finding is marked

Each finding shows how well it is supported: confirmed at an official source, reported by several independent sites, resting on a single source, or the developer’s own claim. Where sources disagree, each version is shown side by side.

What we do not do

We do not fill a gap with a guess. What could not be found is said so plainly, with who to ask. We do not sum a project up in a number, and a report never says whether to buy.

The developer’s reply

Any developer may reply to any finding. The reply appears beside the finding and never replaces it.
