## Appendix A: The sales-office card

Keep this on your phone. Ask each question in writing, and record the date of the answer. A good answer comes with a document and a date. A weak answer is a promise.

**Who is selling**

1. Which exact legal entity signs my agreement and receives my money?
2. Who owns the land, and what gives you the right to sell it?
3. Which projects have you completed, and what were their promised and actual dates?

**Approvals**

4. Which approvals cover my phase, with dates and expiry?
5. What is the project's registration number with the regulator?
6. What is the total approved area, and how does it compare with the homes on sale?

**The agreement**

7. May I take the full agreement and every schedule home before I pay more than a refundable booking amount?
8. Which promises made here are written into the agreement?
9. How far can the plans, the area or the specification change without my consent?

**The money**

10. What is the all-in cost on one page, with every charge and tax?
11. Is the payment plan tied to dates or to certified stages?
12. Which account do I pay into, and will every payment appear in a receipt and in the agreement?

**Time**

13. What is the possession date, as a month and year in the agreement, and what counts as possession?
14. What do I receive for each month of delay, and when may I cancel and be refunded?
15. What percentage of the work is certified complete, and by whom?

**The build**

16. What is the carpet area, and how is it measured?
17. Where is the specification listed, by brand, grade and thickness?
18. May I stand in a finished, empty unit, and may my own inspector visit?

**Your life**

19. In which phase is each amenity delivered, and what will each cost me each year?
20. What has been approved for the land and roads around the project?

**After the purchase**

21. Which law governs the agreement, and where are disputes heard?
22. When does the defect liability period start and end?
23. When will the title be registered in my name, and when does the owners' association take over?

**For yourself**

24. Who gains if I say yes today?
25. What would make me regret this in five years?

## Appendix B: Words you will hear

Markets use different names for the same things, so ask for the local definition each time.

- **Apostille:** a certificate that authenticates a public document so that another country can recognise it, in countries that accept it.
- **As-built drawings:** drawings of the building as it was actually built, showing where pipes and cables run.
- **Booking or token amount:** money paid to reserve a home before the agreement is signed. Whether it is refundable depends on its terms.
- **Built-up area:** the carpet area plus the thickness of the walls, balconies and similar spaces.
- **Carpet or net internal area:** the floor inside your walls.
- **Charge or lien:** a legal claim over a property, often held by a lender, that must be cleared for the owner to have a clean title.
- **Completion or occupancy certificate:** a document, under various names, issued by the authority to confirm that a building was completed in line with its approvals and may be occupied.
- **Defect liability period:** the time after handover during which the seller must repair defects.
- **Designated or escrow account:** an account that holds buyer money for a project and releases it against stated conditions.
- **Encumbrance:** a claim or burden on a property, such as a mortgage.
- **Force majeure:** events beyond a party's control that the agreement says excuse a delay.
- **Freehold and leasehold:** freehold ownership has no fixed end. Leasehold is the right to use the property for a fixed period.
- **Grace period:** extra time after the promised date during which the seller is not treated as late.
- **Handover:** the point at which the seller gives you possession.
- **Loading factor:** the share of the saleable area that lies outside your walls (Chapter 7).
- **Loan-to-value:** the loan as a percentage of the price or value of the property.
- **Owners' association:** the body of owners, known by several names, that runs the common areas after handover.
- **Planning fallacy:** the habit of underestimating how long a project will take (Chapter 6).
- **Possession:** usually the date you receive the home to use. The agreement defines it.
- **Power of attorney:** a document that authorises another person to act for you.
- **Property regulator:** a public body that oversees property sales and developers in some markets, such as RERA in India.
- **Reference-class forecast:** an estimate based on how comparable past projects actually performed.
- **Saleable or super built-up area:** the built-up area plus your share of the common spaces.
- **Service or maintenance charge:** what owners pay to run and maintain the building.
- **Sinking or reserve fund:** money collected from owners for major future repairs.
- **Snag or snagging:** a defect noted at handover and listed for repair.
- **Stage-linked and time-linked plans:** in a stage-linked plan you pay when a defined stage of construction is complete. In a time-linked plan you pay on set dates, whatever has been built.
- **Strata:** ownership of a unit in a building, together with a share of the common property.
- **Title:** the legal right of ownership. A clean title has no competing claims.

## Appendix C: The buyer's risk register

Professionals manage a large purchase with a risk register. This one is adapted from the process in ISO 31000, the international risk-management standard. It takes an hour to complete and it changes how you decide.

**The five steps**

1. **Identify.** List what could go wrong, using the chapters of this guide.
2. **Analyse.** For each risk, rate how likely it is (1 to 5) and how bad it would be (1 to 5).
3. **Evaluate.** Multiply the two ratings. As a rule of thumb, a rating of 15 or more needs an action before you pay anything non-refundable. A rating of 8 to 14 needs an action or a decision to accept the risk knowingly. Below 8, note it and watch it.
4. **Treat.** Reduce the risk (ask, verify, negotiate), transfer it (insurance, a contract clause), avoid it (do not buy), or accept it knowingly.
5. **Monitor.** Review the register at every payment, and whenever something changes.

**An example.** The ratings below are illustrations. Yours will differ.

| Risk | Likelihood (1–5) | Impact (1–5) | Rating | What I will do | By when | Done |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The completion date slips | 4 | 4 | 16 | Write the twelve-months-late plan; ask for an exit and refund clause | Before signing | ☐ |
| The seller entity is not the brand | 3 | 5 | 15 | Verify the legal name and registration number; sign only with that entity | Before booking | ☐ |
| The loan rate rises two points | 3 | 4 | 12 | Run the stress test; ask about a fixed rate | Before the loan | ☐ |
| The approvals do not cover my phase | 2 | 5 | 10 | Ask for each approval; check the regulator's register | Before booking | ☐ |
| My currency falls 10% | 3 | 3 | 9 | Ask about fixing the rate for known payments | Before the first payment | ☐ |
| An amenity arrives after my family needs it | 4 | 2 | 8 | Run the age-at-possession sums | Before signing | ☐ |

**Your register.** Copy the table and add a row for every risk that matters to your family: the neighbour's land, the school catchment, the lease you must end, the parent who may move in. A register that fits your life is worth more than a longer one that does not.
