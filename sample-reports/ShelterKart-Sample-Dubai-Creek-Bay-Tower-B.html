What to ask, and who

Before booking

  • Sales team

    “Please show me this tower's DLD project registration number and its entry on the Dubai REST app or Dubai Pulse.”

    Good answer: A specific project number that can be looked up independently.

    Red flag: ‘It's registered, don't worry’ with no number given.

Before signing

  • Legal team

    “Has Emaar missed a published handover date on any comparable Dubai Creek Harbour tower, and if so, what happened to buyers' payments?”

    Good answer: A specific, named example with a factual outcome.

    Red flag: A flat denial with no specific example addressed.

Before each payment

  • Your bank

    “Which escrow account is this payment going into, and can I get RERA's own escrow account confirmation for this specific project?”

    Good answer: A named escrow account with RERA confirmation.

    Red flag: Any account not confirmed as the project's registered escrow account.

Before possession

  • Your own snagging inspector

    “Can an independent snagging inspection be done before handover, and how long does Emaar commit to fixing what it finds?”

    Good answer: A written snagging process with repair timelines.

    Red flag: “Any issues can be raised after you move in.”

Your protections as a buyer

For Dubai. Verified 2026-09-30. General information about buyers’ rights, not legal advice for your case. Laws change: check the current position with a lawyer before you act.

  • Your right

    Pay only into the project’s escrow account

    Every payment for an off-plan home must go into that project’s own escrow account at a bank approved by the Dubai Land Department, released to the developer only as construction milestones are verified. Check the project and its escrow account on the Dubai REST app before paying, and never pay into any other account.

    Dubai Law No. 8 of 2007 (escrow accounts) · Sources: Mondaq (HAS Law Firm) · Afridi & Angell · BSA Ahmad Bin Hezeem

  • Your right

    Your purchase must be registered (Oqood)

    Every off-plan sale must be recorded on the Land Department’s interim register, known as Oqood. If the developer took your 4% registration fee but did not register the sale, you may seek an order to register it, a refund of the fee, damages or, where justified, cancellation. Ask for your Oqood certificate.

    Dubai Law No. 13 of 2008 (interim property register) · Sources: Mondaq · Afridi & Angell · BSA Ahmad Bin Hezeem

  • Budget for

    Land Department fee: 4% of the price

    The Dubai Land Department fee is 4% of the price, plus smaller administration and trustee fees. Some launches waive or share it, so get any waiver in writing in your Sale and Purchase Agreement.

    Dubai Land Department fees · Sources: Sotheby’s Realty · Mondaq

  • If it goes wrong

    If the handover is late

    Not every delay entitles you to cancel: your Sale and Purchase Agreement and RERA’s process decide, including the reasons for the delay. Keep every payment record and the Oqood registration, and take legal advice before stopping payments.

    Dubai Law No. 13 of 2008; RERA (Dubai) · Sources: Mondaq (HAS Law Firm) · BSA Ahmad Bin Hezeem
